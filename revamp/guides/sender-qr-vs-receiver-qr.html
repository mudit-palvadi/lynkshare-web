Pairing

Sender QR vs Receiver QR.

LynkShare has two QR directions. They answer one question: who is holding the screen that the other person needs to scan? Choosing the right direction removes typing and makes the transfer feel immediate.

Sender QR

The sender picks the files, chooses Cloud or Direct, and shows a QR code. The receiver scans it with the camera. If the receiver is not in the same room, the sender can share the same link remotely instead.

  1. Sender chooses files and a transfer mode.
  2. Sender shows the sender QR or sends the link.
  3. Receiver scans or opens it.
  4. Receiver saves the file.

Use this when the sender starts the transfer, when the receiver is remote, or when the link needs to be sent through a chat.

Receiver QR

The receiver opens the receiving screen and shows a QR code. The sender scans it from the Android app, which tells LynkShare which device is waiting. The sender then sends the file to that device.

  1. Receiver opens the receiving screen and shows the QR.
  2. Sender scans it from the app.
  3. Sender sends the chosen files to the waiting receiver.
  4. Receiver saves the file.

Use this when the receiver is already waiting, when the receiver cannot easily scan (for example, a laptop showing the QR), or when you want the sender to target a specific device. The receiving-QR path uses Cloud delivery.

Which one should I use?

SituationUse
Receiver is remote and will open a link laterSender QR or link
Both people are in the same roomEither; receiver QR is fastest for “send to this device”
Receiver is on a laptop without a cameraReceiver QR; the phone scans the laptop screen
Sender wants DirectSender QR, then choose Direct
Receiver is on iPhone/iPadSender QR/link; the browser receives through Cloud

Keep the QR private

A QR contains the same capability as the link. Do not put it on a public screen or in a public issue. If a code is rejected or a link expires, ask for a fresh transfer.
